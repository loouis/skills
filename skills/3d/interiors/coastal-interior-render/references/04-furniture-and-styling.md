# Furniture, upholstery and styling

## Product-led modelling

When the user asks to choose furniture, show real product references before modelling. Do not substitute a search for downloadable generic meshes. Once a product is selected, preserve its identity and variant; propose a replacement only for a concrete reason.

Gather official front, side, rear, three-quarter and dimension drawings. Record which dimensions are published and which profiles are estimates. Match silhouette first, then support structure, upholstery and seams. A manufacturer mesh is not proof of the right scale, finish or assembly; a photo reconstruction is not factory CAD.

For multipart imports: link every part, update the dependency graph, capture all original world matrices, then apply one group placement. Do not capture transforms after some parts have already moved. Confirm source units by dimensions, not filename: prior assets mixed centimetres and millimetres. Check dependency/parent relationships, hidden parts, material slots and relative positions. A previously displaced plant soil insert demonstrates why the whole assembly must be reviewed.

## Stool direction and selected reference

The selected exemplar was Poliform Sophie Lite in tan nubuck, dark elm-like timber and muted brown-nickel metal. The published low variant is 550 W×550 D×850 H mm with a 630 mm seat. Do not uniformly shorten the tall version: seat/back, frame and footrest relationships need the correct variant.

Reconstruct the curved continuous upholstered shell, lowered arm ends, back aperture, seat cushion, supported carrier, tapered legs and continuous footrest. Avoid blocky arm tips, a protruding carrier, disconnected rear cushion or flat inflated shell. Follow each timber member with grain and apply metal reflections to the ring without turning it into glowing white wire.

Review an isolated three-quarter view, orthographic front/side, rear, tailoring detail and both full stools in the room. Verify the carrier actually supports the seat, all four footrest joints meet their legs and floor glides rest on the floor. Nearest-point contact at one spot is not proof of a resolved joint. The old post-tip overlap gate belonged to a different stool and must not be reused uncritically for this shell.

Nubuck needs broad calm colour, fine nap and changing grazing response. Strong mottling reads dirty; large bump reads sandy; uniform matte brown reads cardboard. See material settings in [the recipe](10-selected-recipe.md). Leave unseen or unpublished factory details labelled as estimates.

## Sofa form before fabric

The source workflow adapted a licensed manufacturer sofa mesh and added custom arm/base/support details. Do not distribute that mesh with this skill. For a new scene, obtain a suitable licensed asset or build original geometry. Preserve source versus custom finish attribution.

Judge five distinct layers:

1. Broad fill and silhouette: weight, arm curvature, seat sag, back lean, cushion volume.
2. Compression and tension: cushions press into supporting surfaces; broad asymmetry precedes tiny wrinkles.
3. Seam construction: panel joins, covered welts and restrained piping, with coherent ends.
4. Fabric: physically sized warp/weft and subtle nap, not giant repeated grooves.
5. Light: highlights move across curves and fold valleys retain soft contact shadow.

Rounded boxes with perfect corners fail even under a detailed weave. Deep gathers from a sculptural accent chair should not be copied onto a tailored cream sofa. Random dent noise is not compression. Add a few meaningful creases around loaded corners and seams; soften isolated sharp grooves that look cut into foam.

Inspect the uncovered arm as well as the dressed sofa. Throws can hide a bad corner, open seam or exposed frame. Re-seat pillows after changing cushion geometry. The preferred accent was a smaller muted terracotta cushion; choose dimensions against the actual back/seat, not the empty camera frame.

## Modest wooden lift

A tested revision lifted the sofa only 40 mm using six short tapered timber supports, with about 7 mm hidden engagement into the base. The seat crown ended near 455 mm. These are example proportions, not product dimensions. Raise all relevant parts together, including loose cushions and seam geometry; then update the drape at contact zones.

Audit feet against actual rug yarn geometry and add plausible felt/contact compression. Do not leave glides above the visible yarn crown or bury the legs deeply. Preserve horizontal footprint and circulation. Check a low support camera; a hero shadow cannot establish grounded feet.

## Coffee table and objects

Use a low sculptural table with believable top thickness, edge easing and a supportive base. Molteni Aster was a sculptural table reference; the developed table was custom geometry, not manufacturer CAD. The preferred developed direction used smoked oak; quiet travertine and grey marble were tested separately. Keep geometry and camera identical during material comparisons. Grey marble may compete with the island; this is a composition judgement, not a banned material.

Map wood on the top AND vertical edge, with grain aligned to actual fabrication. Avoid turning all vertical UVs by object height. Support the table on the rug and include the full edge in clearance checks.

Two art books work better than several generic block props when they have correct format, cover boards, spine, page block, slight cover lip and a restrained offset. Use licensed/public-domain or original cover art for distributed outputs; a publisher product photo is a reference, not automatically redistributable texture. Historical Albers/Matisse references supplied cover-format dimensions, while thickness was estimated. Record that distinction rather than inventing exact editions.

Complement with one shallow metal/ceramic dish and a small smoke-glass vessel or votive. Model glass thickness and base contact. Vary height without making a museum display. Avoid objects blocking reach, walk-through or the hero island silhouette.

## Plants

Capture whole-asset transforms before scaling. Inspect pot, soil, trunk and foliage together; make soil offset proportional to actual pot size instead of reusing a fixed distance. Keep leaf density and branch intersections believable. Check the plant silhouette from every main camera: a foreground plant can unintentionally block the appliance or island.

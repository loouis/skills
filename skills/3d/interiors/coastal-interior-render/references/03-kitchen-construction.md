# Kitchen construction and functional checks

## Island and stone

Build the slab, ends, carcass, support and recess as an assembly. In the established direction, the island is 3200×1050 mm, finished 900 mm high, with a 70 mm top and matching 70 mm end thickness. The seating side has a 400 mm recess. Treat these as a starting design, not structural advice.

1. Apply scale deliberately and measure the evaluated upper/lower surfaces. Confirm the top is horizontal in world space. Perspective makes far edges slope on screen; measure geometry rather than rotating the top until it looks horizontal in the image.
2. Keep equal visible thickness through curved ends and corners. A bevel should not consume most of a thin part.
3. Distinguish free exposed arrises from bonded waterfall mitres. Rounding both mating edges creates an apparent open joint. A tested example used 1.6 mm easing on exposed arrises and only 0.056 mm at the bonded mitre inside the same 70 mm envelope. These are modelling values, not fabrication tolerances.
4. Map stone in slab space with deliberate cuts/vein continuity. After Boolean openings, verify the active UV layer and every exposed cut face. Avoid triplanar drift that makes grain or veining turn arbitrarily.
5. Inspect from the hero, a corner close-up and above. Check both ends, bottom shadow, seating recess, sink opening and countertop support. One attractive end cannot clear the other.

## Sink and tap: define the working side

Label the cook's standing zone before placement. Mount the tap on the far rim relative to the cook with its outlet over the bowl, unless a different functional arrangement is explicitly designed. Do not infer correct placement from a compressed hero camera.

- Measure faucet base, outlet and bowl centre against this local orientation. The old scene used cook y<1.90 m, tap y=2.47 m and bowl/outlet y=2.22 m; these are a coordinate example only. Define new coordinates from the new plan.
- Model a real bowl below the worktop, clear the carcass beneath it and verify that rays through the opening reach the bowl floor/waste. A hole in the stone is insufficient.
- Include a supported mounting flange, connected lever, spout outlet and consistent section. Inspect the complete basin from above and the working aisle.
- Use enough radial segments for the final crop. Check silhouette and reflection bands for faceting; the previous approximately 26 micrometre chord-error screen was a local quality test, not a universal tessellation target.
- Keep a gentle floor-to-waste fall. Converging radial topology can make a sharp reflected star or pinched floor despite smooth shading. Use reasonably spaced loops and inspect reflections after subdivision.
- Keep stone contact, seal/flange, bowl rim, wall thickness and drainage continuity plausible. A visual sink is not a plumbing specification.

## Joinery

Default rear bank: six drawer fronts arranged two columns × three rows, consistent 3 mm reveals and discreet custom underside/edge pulls. Do not revert to a grid of many small fronts and long bar handles. Model side/end panels and the counter joint so the edge does not become a large unexplained gap.

The historical handle study used Furnipart Edge Straight as a discreet edge-pull reference. The modelled pull remained a custom proposal, not an exact product specification.

The reveal is not the pull. Show a lip/rebate that fingers can actually use and a connected fixing/return. Test approach from the standing position. Inspect the top row against the slab and the last column against the side panel. Include handles in aisle measurements. Vertical veneer may continue deliberately within a panel family, but avoid a single seamless grain field running through all separate fronts and appliances.

Integrate fridge/freezer, dishwasher and pantry where the plan supports it. Appliance doors need hinge/slide logic, landing space, ventilation and service access. Keep unresolved product-specific requirements in the review ledger.

## Appliances

The style uses a coordinated oven/combi-steam, flush induction, concealed extraction, integrated refrigeration and dishwasher suite. Gaggenau was the visual reference. BO471112 was a discontinued Australian visual reference; never silently specify it as a current product. Recheck official current dimensions and installation documents for a real product selection.

Build more than a black rectangle: outer fascia and returns, inset glass, cavity depth, shelves/racks where visible, gasket and handle, machined selectors, markings and coherent proportions. Confirm left/right controls from the actual front camera: object-space orientation can be misleading.

For a flush hob, build a closed mesh with the display region truly coplanar. Inspect all four corners in a dedicated camera. A raised or open TFT can disappear in a shallow hero while remaining wrong. Represent discreet ventilation/extraction rather than treating the entire backsplash as uninterrupted decoration; a prior custom hood used 72 baffles, but the count is not a style requirement.

Pale appliance glass is not automatically missing geometry. At a shallow angle it may reflect the ceiling. Use a temporary zero-specular/source-off control to identify this, then restore the material. Do not leave a diffuse-black diagnostic in the selected scene to conceal a reflection problem.

## Small joins and styling

Fruit needs restrained variation in size, tilt, skin roughness and stem position. Stem roots should enter the fruit, not hover above it; a previous approximately 1.8 mm burial was a visual modelling choice. Repeated identical roots and perfect grids still look artificial after connection tests pass. Bowls need thickness and surface contact; branches should grow from connected stems and enter the vase plausibly. Preserve prep space and keep decor out of the sink working zone.

## Required kitchen evidence

Render hero, kitchen detail, front joinery, stone corner, full sink overhead, sink working side, appliance front and all-corners cooktop. Record level/thickness, reveals, grips, hardware aisle, bowl/waste clearance, faucet support/outlet and appliance face orientation. If only a local detail changed, first verify it locally; before delivery include every affected reflection and room view.
